Machines and people

Two things are on this page:

  • Your own other machines. Sessions on another Mac, a Linux host or a VM of yours join your voice: you hear their reports, send to them, approve their prompts, and always see which machine each one is on.

  • A shared machine. The owner of a machine invites other people to it. Each person adds the machine to their own voice, and talks to its sessions from their own call, with their own keys. The owner keeps their own voice too.

The designs and the reasons: design/multi-machine.md and design/people-and-machines.md.

Words

The docs and the screens say "your voice", "machines" and "people". The command line keeps its older words: every pointe hub ... and pointe node ... command still works.

The docs and the screens say Meaning Before, and still in the commands
Your voice One person's Pointe: the call, the brain, the notes and the model keys. It runs on a Mac or a Linux machine The hub (pointe hub ...)
Machine A computer whose sessions a voice can reach: the one your voice runs on, another one of yours, or one somebody invited you to A node (pointe node ...), or the hub itself
Person Somebody the owner of a machine invited to it. A person reaches the machine from their own voice Did not exist
Owner Of a machine: the person whose Pointe runs on it. Of a session: the person who started it from their voice, else the owner of the machine it runs on Implied

Your own other machines

Your sessions do not all run on the machine with your voice. Pair your other machines with it, and their sessions join the same voice. Each other machine runs a small Pointe (pointe node ...) with no microphone, no speaker, no models and no keys. It watches its own sessions, types your requests into them and holds their permission prompts.

Set it up

  1. On the machine with your voice, make it reachable, as for the phone: pointe pair tailscale, pointe pair wifi or pointe pair host <name>. The other machine reaches the same listener the phone does.

  2. Make a one-time code there, in any of three places:

    • pointe hub invite in a terminal. It prints the whole line to paste.
    • The page: Settings → Machines → Invite a machine.
    • The iPhone app: Settings → Machines → Invite a machine. The app asks for Face ID before it shows the code, and shows the whole line to copy.

    The code works once and lasts 10 minutes. Cancel it from the same place.

  3. On the other machine, paste the line:

    pointe node join 'pointe://hub?v=1&host=…&port=7443&fp=…&code=K7PQ-2MXD'

    The other machine checks your voice's certificate against the fingerprint in the line before it sends anything. It then says which vendors your voice uses, because its sessions' transcripts go there.

  4. Check the pairing. Your voice shows the machine's name, address and key fingerprint, for example "devbox paired from 100.101.5.7 (Tailscale), key 3f2a…". pointe node status on the other machine shows the same fingerprint. If they differ, revoke it.

  5. Join sessions on the other machine as you do on the first: /pointe:voice in Claude Code, pointe join opencode or pointe join codex in a folder.

A Linux machine needs uv and ps (procps): pointe node join checks and says what to install. A machine paired this way does not install the voice models: on Linux, bash install.sh --node installs it without them.

Use it

On the screens. With one machine, nothing changes. With more than one, on the page and in the app:

  • The sessions are grouped by machine, this Mac first. Each group shows the machine's OS, its round trip ("23 ms") or "offline since 14:02", and how many sessions need you. On the page a click folds a group; in the app the Sessions sheet has a section per machine.

  • Filter chips at the top: All · studio · devbox · laptop. One tap shows only that machine's sessions and narrows the "Orchestrating N sessions" line. A chip is a view only: it sends nothing and changes no mode.

  • Each session carries a machine tag while more than one machine has sessions.

  • An approval card, and its notification, say "shop · devbox needs approval".
  • In the app, hold a session chip and choose "Sessions on devbox" to open the Sessions sheet on that machine.

By voice. Session names are unique across machines. If a second machine has a session with a name already in use, it gets the machine's name added: "shop devbox". Then:

Say Does
"go to devbox" switches to devbox's session; with several there, asks which one
"tell devbox to pull" sends to devbox's session, or asks which one
"tell everyone on devbox to pull", "everyone except the laptop" sends to that machine's sessions only
"what's going on everywhere", "what's on devbox" answers grouped by machine
"where is shop running" names the machine

A machine's name counts only where it says who something is for ("tell devbox", "on devbox"). "Fix the devbox config" names no session. The voice says the machine when the name alone is not enough, when you switch to another machine, and in "everywhere" answers. It does not repeat it in every report.

When a machine goes offline

A machine that sleeps, or whose network drops, is marked offline within about 12 seconds:

  • Its sessions stay listed, dimmed, with "offline since 14:02". A send to one is refused ("devbox is offline").

  • A prompt it held goes back to its own terminal. Its card says "offline: answer it in its terminal", with no Approve or Deny, and its notification has no actions.

  • You can still focus an offline session and ask what its notes say.

  • When the machine comes back it reconnects by itself and sends the lines you missed. After 10 minutes offline its sessions are closed, as an exited session is.

Approvals

Your voice keeps every rule it has for the sessions on its own machine (SECURITY.md). The other machine checks again before it answers a prompt:

  • A spoken yes counts only if your voice read out exactly the text the other machine computed from the real request, in short or word for word. A request it never reads out (one that holds a credential-like value) cannot be approved by voice.

  • A notification's Approve counts only for a request that machine can read out.

  • A tap on the page or in the app is trusted from your voice, as a tap on its own sessions is. What the card shows is the request with credentials masked; the exact request is in the session's own terminal.

  • A no from anywhere denies.

To answer unreadable prompts only in that machine's terminal, set "remote_taps": "readable_only" in its ~/.claude-voice/config.json. Then a tap can approve only what could have been read out. /pointe:doctor on that machine says which setting is on.

What goes where

  • To your voice: each session's transcript, with credentials masked on the machine it runs on; its name, folder path, process id and agent; up to 40 file names; permission requests in masked form and in read-out form. When you look for sessions to add (features/add-sessions.md): each listed session's name, folder, agent, age, title, keywords and first and last request, cut to 160 characters, and nothing of a session you do not add after that.

  • From your voice: your requests to its sessions, and your answers to its prompts.

  • Never: vendor keys (the other machine has none), your voiceprint, the journal, session notes, the dictionary, the page or phone keys, or the machine's own key (it proves the key with a fresh challenge each time).

Your voice treats the other machine's transcripts like its own: they go to its reader and brain vendors. pointe node join and pointe node status name those vendors. A voice in fully local mode (pointe local) sends them to none.

Your own machines, each a full voice

Each of your machines can run its own full Pointe, with its own keys, and your phone, Mac app and page can call any of them directly (iPhone app, Several machines, design). They can also link each other, so the one you call reaches the rest:

  1. On one machine: pointe machines link kite prints a one-time line (kite is the name the other machine gets here).
  2. On the other machine: pointe machine add '<line>'. Do the same the other way round for the second direction.

A machine linked this way is yours, not a person's: it sees every session there, private ones and agents included (never another machine's: links do not pass sessions on), what it sends carries your own tag and is never refused as busy against you, a permission prompt is offered to it at once, and its reports are spoken as your own. An approval is taken once, on the machine the session runs on, whichever of your voices answered it; the others are told it was answered.

Two of your voices on somebody else's machine. If Avi invited you and you add avi-box to a second voice of yours too, ask Avi for pointe people invite <your name> --another-voice: that voice gets its own key under your one name, so the two never count as two people (busy, "the turn you started", who answered).

A shared machine

One machine, one OS account, several people. The owner's Pointe runs there and is the owner's voice. Each other person runs their own Pointe on their own Mac or Linux machine, and adds the shared machine to it. Nobody shares a key or a call.

Read this first. A person you invite can make the machine's agents run commands as the account Pointe runs as, and a session Pointe starts skips permission prompts by default (New session). Pointe does not isolate people from each other on one account. Invite only people you would give a shell on that account. The whole trust model: SECURITY.md.

The examples use Avi, who owns the machine avi-box, and Dana, whom he invites.

The owner invites a person

The machine must be reachable first, as for a phone (pointe pair tailscale, pointe pair wifi or pointe pair host <name>): a person's voice connects to the same listener, port 7443. Without an address the invite says how to set one up.

Avi makes an invite in any of four places:

Where How
A terminal pointe people invite dana, or pointe people invite dana --watch for watch only. It prints the line, waits, and says who paired
The page, on the machine itself Settings → Machines → Invite a person: a name, then Can talk to the sessions or Can only watch, then Show the invite
The iPhone app Settings → Machines → People → Invite a person. The app asks for Face ID first
The Mac app The same screen as the iPhone's. It asks for Touch ID or the password first

The invite is one line and, on the screens, a QR code of the same line:

  pointe://machine?v=1&host=avi-box.example&port=7443&fp=<64 hex>&code=K7PQ-2MXD&name=avi-box

It works once and lasts ten minutes. One invite is out at a time: a new one replaces it. Cancel the invite ends it early. The page shows People only on the machine itself: a page opened in a phone's browser does not.

A person's name is said aloud and stamped on what they send. It may hold letters, digits, spaces, dots and dashes, it must be a name the voice can tell apart, and it cannot be the owner's name or a name already on the machine.

When Dana's voice pairs, the terminal prints two lines, and the apps say "Dana can reach this machine now.":

  dana paired from 100.101.5.9 (Tailscale), key 3f2a…
  dana's voice sends this machine's transcripts to: Deepgram, Gemini.

The person adds the machine

Dana needs her own Pointe with her own model keys, on the same private network as avi-box (Tailscale, Twingate or the LAN). She adds the machine in any of four places:

Where How
A terminal pointe machine add '<line>', with --name NAME to call the machine something else in her voice
The page, on her machine Settings → Machines → "Paste the line the machine's owner sent you" → Add. A pasted line is added at once
The iPhone app Settings → Machines → Add a machine. The camera opens to scan the code on the owner's screen; Paste instead takes the line. The app asks for Face ID before it adds
The Mac app Settings → Machines → Add a machine: paste the line or drop it on the field, then Add. The app asks for Touch ID or the password before it adds

Her voice checks the machine's certificate against the fingerprint in the line before it sends anything, trades the code for her own key on that machine, and connects. There is no account and no second step.

What a refusal says, each in one sentence:

  • The line is not a machine invite, or is cut short.
  • The certificate is not the one in the invite: "ask them for a new invite".
  • The code is wrong, expired or already used.
  • The machine cannot be reached: it must be on the same private network.
  • The machine is her own, or is already in her voice.
  • The machine's name is taken in her voice. The screens then ask for another name under Call it; the command takes --name. A refused name does not use up the invite.

The disclosure, once. After the add, Dana's screens say one time where her voice sends that machine's transcripts, until she presses OK. The page says "Your voice sends the transcripts of the sessions on avi-box, with credentials masked, to: Deepgram, Gemini. avi sees this list next to your name." pointe machine add prints the same fact and the screens then do not repeat it. Avi sees the same list next to Dana's name.

What the person sees and hears

  • avi-box is in Settings → Machines, marked as Avi's, with its state and round trip, like a machine of her own. pointe machine list (or pointe machines) prints every machine in her voice and whose each one is.

  • Its shared sessions are in her one conversation, grouped under avi-box. Each one that is not hers carries its owner's name ("avi's"). A session of her own never carries a name.

  • A Mine chip joins the filter chips while any session in her voice is somebody else's. It shows only her own sessions.

  • She talks to those sessions as to her own: "tell shop to run the tests". The agent on avi-box reads the request with her name on it: "[Voice request from dana, a person invited to this machine, relayed by dana's voice assistant".

  • If a session on avi-box has the same name as one of hers, hers keeps the plain name and the other gets the machine's name added: "shop avi-box".

  • A session that waits on a permission prompt shows that it waits. If she may send, she can answer it once its owner had 20 seconds: see Approving a prompt.

Send or watch

Each person has one grant on a machine:

Grant The person can Set with
send (the default) See the shared sessions and their transcripts, and send requests to them pointe people invite dana, Can talk to the sessions
watch See the shared sessions and their transcripts. The machine refuses every request from them pointe people invite dana --watch, Can only watch

The owner changes it at any time: pointe people grant dana watch or pointe people grant dana send; on the page, one click on the person's Can talk or Watch only button; in the apps, the menu on the person's row. It applies from that moment, and the person's screens show "watch only" on the machine.

The machine enforces the grant, not the person's voice. A request from a person who may only watch is answered "Not sent. avi lets you watch avi-box, not talk to its sessions."

Private sessions

Every session on the shared machine is shared with every person invited to it, unless its owner makes it private.

  • pointe share shop --private keeps shop to the owner. pointe share shop --shared shares it again. With no flag, pointe share shop makes it private.

  • On the page: the lock mark on the session's row, or Private in its ⋯ sheet. In the apps: long-press the session (right-click on the Mac) and choose Private; Shared with people shares it again.

  • The choice is offered on a session the owner owns, on the machine itself, once the machine has people.

A private session leaves every person's voice at once: its name, its transcript and its file names stop crossing, and lines already read for a person are not sent. Shared again, it continues where it stopped. A private session stays private across a restart. There is no voice command for Private yet.

Sessions on the owner's other machines (the ones paired with pointe hub invite) are never passed on to people. Only the shared machine's own sessions are.

Who hears which report

  • You hear the report of a turn you started. Dana asks shop to run the tests; when the turn ends, her voice reads the report, as for a session of her own.

  • A report of a turn somebody else started on somebody else's machine is held. It is not spoken. While a call is up it counts on the screen, and "anything from the others?" reads it. With no call up it is not held: it goes to the session's notes only.

  • The owner's voice is not changed. On the owner's own machine, reports are spoken as before, whoever started the turn.

  • A request another person sent is noted, never taken for yours. When Omer sends shop a request, Dana's voice adds "omer asked shop: …" to the session's notes, and the voice never reports it as her own. The owner's voice shows it in Recent requests as "sent by omer".

A session that is busy

A session takes one request at a time from people. If Dana sends to shop while it works on somebody else's request, the machine refuses it and her voice says, word for word:

Not sent. shop is working on omer's request from 14:02. Say send it anyway, and it goes.

  • A session that works on a turn no invited person asked for (the owner typed it in the terminal, or asked from their own call) is busy with the owner's name.

  • "Send it anyway" delivers the same request once, if she says it within 120 seconds of the refusal. Her voice then says "Sent to shop." It must be her own whole utterance; the model cannot say it for her.

  • "Send it anyway" does not pass the other refusals:

The machine refuses when Her voice says
She may only watch "Not sent. avi lets you watch avi-box, not talk to its sessions."
The session waits on a permission prompt "Not sent. shop waits on a permission prompt on avi-box. It takes a request once that is answered." If you may send, you may answer it: see below
The request says somebody approved something ("Avi said yes") "Not sent. avi-box takes no message that says somebody approved something: an approval comes from the person who gives it."
The session went private "Not sent. avi keeps shop private now."

Approving a prompt on a shared machine

If you may send, you may also answer a session's permission prompt there.

  • The session's owner is asked first: the person who started it, else the machine's owner. You see the request at once, with "avi is asked first", and your voice says nothing yet.

  • After 20 seconds, or at once when the owner is not on a call, your voice reads the request out and your screens offer Approve and Deny. Say yes or no, or tap.

  • The first answer wins. If somebody answered before you, your voice says who.

  • The machine checks your answer itself. A spoken yes counts only if your voice read out exactly what the machine will run.

  • Everybody is told who answered, and the machine keeps a record with your name.

The machine's owner can turn this off: pointe people approvals off. They change the 20 seconds with pointe people window <seconds>.

When the machine's owner is not on a call, a prompt waits 45 seconds for you. Then it goes to its terminal, and your answer no longer counts. The owner changes that with pointe people hold <seconds>.

Starting a session on a shared machine

If you may send, you can start a session there: "start a session on avi-box in the shop folder", or the New session sheet with the machine picked.

  • The folders are the ones the machine's owner works in. Your voice asks the machine for the list.

  • Your voice reads it back with the machine and whose it is: "Start a Claude Code session on avi-box, avi's machine, in shop?" The first time, it also says how that machine starts sessions: "avi-box starts sessions that skip permission prompts: it can edit files and run commands as avi without asking."

  • How the session starts is the owner's choice, not yours.

  • The session is yours. You are asked first for its prompts. You can keep it private with pointe share <session> --private: then you and the machine's owner see it.

The machine's owner can turn this off with pointe people starts off, or make these sessions always ask before actions with pointe people sessions ask.

What the owner sees

  • The People list: Settings → Machines on the page of the machine itself, People in the apps, or pointe people. Each person has a name, a grant, whether their voice is linked now or when it was last here, and the services their voice sends this machine's transcripts to ("Their voice uses: Deepgram, Gemini"). pointe people also prints each person's key fingerprint.

  • The trust line above the list: "People you invite can make these agents run commands as avi. Keep a session to yourself with Private."

  • On a session: "dana is using it" while it works on a person's request, and a lock on a private session.

  • Recent requests on the page lists each person's request as "sent by dana".

  • The journal (~/.claude-voice/journal.jsonl) has a line, with the person's name, for each pairing, link, link that ended, request, refused request, change of grant, change of Private and removal.

  • The owner's name, as people's voices and the agents read it, is "owner" in ~/.claude-voice/config.json, else the name of the OS account.

Remove a person, or a machine

The owner removes a person: pointe people remove dana; on the page, Remove and then Remove dana?; in the apps, Remove in the person's menu, a confirm, then Face ID (Touch ID or the password on the Mac). Only that person's key is deleted and their link closes at once. Nobody else is touched. To come back they need a new invite.

On the person's side the machine stays listed, offline, with "avi-box removed you". Its sessions leave their voice, the voice deletes its key and stops connecting. Remove takes the line off.

A person removes the machine from their voice: pointe machine remove avi-box, or Remove next to the machine in Settings → Machines. Its sessions leave the voice and the voice deletes its key. On the machine the person stays in the People list, not linked, and their key stays valid there until the owner removes them. If the invited person will not come back, the owner should run pointe people remove.

pointe pair rotate and pointe pair off on the shared machine remove every person, and say who to invite again: see SECURITY.md.

What crosses to whom

  • From the shared machine to each linked person's voice: for each shared session, its transcript with credentials masked on the machine, its name, folder path, agent, title, owner, up to 40 file names, and whether it works or waits. Requests other people sent to it, masked, with the sender's name. Its permission prompts, if the person may send and the owner has not turned people's approvals off. Not its process id, not a private session, not a session on another machine.

  • From a person's voice to the shared machine: the person's requests, their answers to prompts, their requests to start a session, the name of the machine their voice runs on, its Pointe version, and the names of the services their voice uses.

  • From each person's voice to that person's vendors: the shared sessions' transcripts, as for their own sessions. Each person's voice uses its own keys. A voice in fully local mode sends them to no outside service.

  • Never: anybody's vendor keys, voiceprint, journal, session notes or dictionary, and no person's key after pairing (each link proves it with a fresh challenge).

Names. Your voice's machine is named by "machine" in its ~/.claude-voice/config.json (else its short host name). Rename it there and restart Pointe: each machine of your own takes the new name at its next link. A machine of your own gets its name when it pairs (pointe hub invite --name devbox); to change it, pair again. A machine somebody invited you to gets its own name, or the one you give with --name.

Versions: an old Pointe next to a new one

Two machines, or a plugin and the Pointe it calls, are often on different versions. Each pair says what it speaks when it connects, and a pair with nothing in common fails in one sentence that names the side to update and the command.

Pair What each side says No common version
A node and its hub The hub's challenge and the node's hello each carry the oldest and newest link protocol they speak. The node's hello and the hub's welcome list the features. The link uses the newest protocol both speak and the features both list The link closes with code 4426. pointe node status on the node says which side is old: "studio runs an older Pointe (0.9.0) than this machine: update it there with pointe update", or "studio needs a newer Pointe on this machine". On the hub the machine stays in Settings → Machines, offline, with "devbox runs an older Pointe (0.9.0). Update it there to see its sessions: pointe update". Nobody's key is deleted, and the node reconnects by itself after the update
A script, hook or command and the Pointe on the same machine X-Pointe-Client: <part>/<version> api=<n> on every request The answer is 426 with {"error", "need", "have", "how"}. The script prints the sentence in error; how is the command. A permission hook that is refused leaves the prompt in the terminal: it never blocks Claude Code
The iPhone app and the Mac The call protocol and the oldest one served, in the status (phone.md) The app says which of the two to update

Every script prints one sentence for any answer that is not what it expects: a refusal, a Pointe that does not know the request, something that is not Pointe. A script from a release before 0.10 sends no header and is served as before; one so old that it fails by itself (the 0.1.6 plugin) is named by pointe doctor, with the commands that update or remove it.

What is tested. A node as 0.9.0 sends its hello (one protocol, no range, no features) links with this hub. The requests the scripts of 0.1.6, 0.8.0, 0.8.7, 0.9.0, 0.10.0, 0.10.1 and 0.10.2 send are replayed against this daemon on every push (tests/fixtures/skew_requests.json): each gets a 200 or a refusal with a sentence. A pair further apart than that is not tested.

Limits

  • A watch-only person cannot answer a prompt or start a session. They see that the session waits.

  • No adding a session that already runs on somebody else's machine. You start a new one there, or its owner joins it.

  • No starting a session on a machine of your own that is a node. Start it there and join it.

  • A request while a prompt waits is refused, and so is a request that says somebody approved something.
  • The settings for people are for everybody on the machine, not one per person.
  • No voice command for Private, a grant, an invite or a removal. Use the screens or the commands.

  • A machine that is only a node serves one voice. A machine paired with pointe node join has no voice of its own and cannot invite people. To share a machine, run Pointe on it as its owner's voice.

  • One voice per machine, one call per voice. Each person's voice needs its own Mac or Linux machine, awake for their call, with their own model keys. A phone or a browser paired with a voice acts as that voice's owner; a second call to the same voice is refused while one is up.

  • One link per person. A person's key links one voice at a time to the machine.

  • Fleet size is measured with typed words and stand-in sessions only. The fleet benchmark (bench/fleet/RESULTS.md) puts up to 10 machines with 30 sessions each on one voice, and two machines with Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode sessions together, on one host: no speech, no real agents, no machines on other networks. How many people one machine serves well is not measured.

  • Not isolation. People on a shared machine act as one OS account (SECURITY.md).

  • Held reports need a call. A report of a turn somebody else started is held for "anything from the others?" only while a call is up.

  • One voice per machine of your own. To move a machine paired with pointe node join to another voice, pointe node leave, then join the new one.

  • No call on a machine paired with pointe node join. The call, its page and the app are on the machine with your voice. /pointe:open on the other machine says where the call is.

  • No launch mode there. pointe start (a Claude Code channel) works only on the machine with your voice. On another machine, use /pointe:voice or pointe join.

  • Adding sessions on a machine of your own works by voice and on screen: "connect to the pentest session on devbox". A closed Claude Code session there is not reopened from your voice (it needs a terminal on that machine); Codex and OpenCode ones are. The read-back says when such a session's approvals stay in its terminal; after it joins, its approvals capability shows as that machine's adapter reports it.

  • "By the way" questions about a session on another machine are answered from its transcript, not from a forked copy.

  • Show while it says does not show files from another machine yet, and Challenge me leaves another machine's diffs out of its evidence and says so.

  • Wi-Fi only (pointe pair wifi): when the address of the machine with your voice changes, pair again and make a new code, as for the phone.

  • Windows cannot be a machine in a voice.
