The Mac app

Pointe in the menu bar, with a native window that has everything the page and the iPhone app have: the call and its transcript, approvals, sessions by machine, the brain dump, challenge, meetings, briefings, whisper, the voice lock, the dictionary, the conflict alarm, show while it says, machines, pairing a phone, and Settings. The window draws the iPhone app's own screens, so the two apps look and behave the same; a wide window adds the page's sessions column. It talks to the same Pointe on this Mac that the page does, so the two can be used side by side (one call at a time). What each surface has, and why the few differences exist: design/parity.md. The Mac's own parts (the protocol, the audio, the shortcut, security): design/macos-app.md.

Getting it

Download Pointe-<version>.dmg from the newest release, drag Pointe to Applications, and open it. The app is signed with Ballerine's Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the ticket stapled, and so is the disk image: macOS opens them without a warning. You need macOS 15 or newer. On a Mac with no Pointe yet, the window is the setup: it installs Pointe with the install.sh built into it (checked against a SHA-256 the app carries, and installing only the release of the app's own version, checked against the SHA-256 pinned in that script), then asks one question, takes your key and starts your first call: Install, Mac. An existing Claude Code plugin or standalone install is found and used; the app never installs a second copy.

Build it from source instead, from the plugin you installed. In Claude Code, /pointe:setup apps does it for you. In a terminal, one command builds it, signs it, and puts it in ~/Applications:

pointe apps mac

You need Xcode 16 or newer (free in the App Store) and macOS 15 or newer. You do not need an Apple Account. pointe apps doctor checks the Mac first and prints the fix for anything missing. The plugin must be installed too (see the README): built this way, the app starts and talks to the plugin's daemon, and pointe is the command setup puts on your PATH. Contributors building from a clone run bin/pointe apps mac there.

What the command does, and why

Step Why
Checks Xcode The build needs the full Xcode, selected and with its license accepted; the command line tools alone cannot build an app.
Chooses how to sign With an Apple Development certificate in your keychain (a free Apple Account is enough, how) it signs as your team. Without one it signs ad hoc, which Xcode calls "Sign to Run Locally". If signing as your team fails, it says why and signs ad hoc instead.
Builds Release The optimized build, into ~/.claude-voice/apps/build (in a clone, apps/macos/build/apps, which git ignores). The first build took 44 s on an Apple-silicon Mac in the clean-install run.
Quits a running Pointe and replaces ~/Applications/Pointe.app So there is one copy, where Spotlight and Login Items find it. Only the copy at that path is quit; if it was running, the new one is opened.

Both ways of signing give an app that runs on this Mac. The difference is the microphone permission: macOS ties it to the signature. Signed as your team, the permission is kept across rebuilds. Signed ad hoc, macOS asks again after each rebuild. With a team, the app's bundle ID is your own (the saved iPhone bundle ID plus .mac), for the same reason as on the iPhone: io.ballerine.pointe.mac belongs to the team that publishes Pointe.

Gatekeeper does not stop it. Gatekeeper checks files that were downloaded (macOS marks them as quarantined), and an app built on this Mac carries no such mark. A copy of your build sent to another Mac is refused there: build on each Mac.

Start at login: in the app, Settings, General, "Open Pointe at login". macOS lists it under System Settings, General, Login Items, where you can remove it.

Options: --open opens the app when it is installed, --adhoc signs to run locally even with a team, --dest <folder> installs somewhere other than ~/Applications, --dry-run shows the Xcode command without running it. Run the command again to update. If the build fails, the command prints the cause and the fix; the table in The iPhone app covers the Xcode license, xcode-select and signing errors, and the full output is in build.log in the build folder.

For work on the app itself, apps/macos/build.sh --open makes a Debug build in apps/macos/build/dd and installs nothing.

Using it

  • The menu bar item is the Chorus mark, dimmed with no call, with the number of sessions that wait beside it. Its menu has Connect, who needs you, Brain dump, Open Pointe, New Session, the call bubble and Settings.

  • The window (Open Pointe, ⌘O). Wide, it has the sessions on the left, grouped by machine, with Orchestrator or Session over them and New session and Settings under them; click a session to make it the one the voice talks to, right-click it to rename it or take it off the voice. Narrow, the sessions are a strip over the conversation, as on the phone. The conversation is on the right; click something you said to fix it. A request shows its exact text with Deny and Approve. Approve asks for Touch ID or your password; Deny does not.

  • Opening the app from Finder, Spotlight or the Dock opens the window, even when the menu bar item is hidden behind the notch. Opened at login, it stays in the menu bar.

  • Connect (⌘↩): the first time, macOS asks for the microphone. A call that does not start opens the window, where Connect says why and what to do.

  • The talk mode is named at the top of the window and in the bubble: Always on, or in push to talk the shortcut, as in "Hold ⌃⌥Space".

  • Talking. The switch over the mic chooses Always on or push to talk. In push to talk, hold the mic, or hold Space while the window is in front, or hold the shortcut from any app (Settings → This Mac → Talk shortcut). Choose Tap to talk in Settings to press once to start and again to send. With Always on, the mic, Space and the shortcut mute.

  • AirPods (during a call): a stem press turns the mic off and on, with the system's own chime; in push to talk it talks. The window shows "AirPods: press for mic off" beside the talk mode, and the bubble shows an AirPods glyph. Play/pause from other headsets and the keyboard's ⏯ key mutes Pointe by default; Settings → This Mac → Play/pause in a call can make it Skip, or Nothing. Outside a call Pointe takes neither, so the music keeps them. Mac app only. Details: features/mute.md.

  • Type (⌘T) to send words instead of speaking; typed words never approve.

  • Brain dump (⌘B on a call): talk freely, press Done, then edit the structured prompt, choose where it goes, and press Send twice.

  • New Session (⌘N): start a Claude Code, OpenCode or Codex session in a folder you already work in. Start asks for Touch ID or your password. A start the voice read back shows as a card with Cancel and Start. features/new-session.md.

  • Settings (⌘, or the ••• button): the same list as the iPhone app's (the microphone, the talk button, how long to wait before answering, Clean up my words, whisper, the dictionary, sessions and mode, machines, Phone, meetings, briefings, challenge, alerts, the voice lock, the conflict alarm, show while it says, appearance, About), then this Mac's own: the talk shortcut, the skip shortcut, play/pause in a call, the call bubble, open at login, Pointe on this Mac (running or not, its version, Start and Restart), the folders a new session may start in, and the model keys: the Setup menu (pointe setup <id>, the rows of setups.md), one line on whether every role is filled, and each key as In use, Saved, not used, Needed (the one key an empty role needs) or Optional, never "missing" when no role needs it (KeyPlan, the daemon's vendors.key_status). Add… opens Pointe's own key window, then pointe key check tries the key with one tiny live call; the app never sees a key.

  • Phone (Settings, or Call → Pair a Phone…): the paired phones, and Connect your phone, which shows the code the iPhone scans, as the page's Settings → Phone does.

  • The Call menu has a key for each: Mute (⇧⌘A, in Always on), Approve Request (⌘Y, with Touch ID or the password, as the card's button) and Deny Request (⌘D) for the first card that waits, Sessions and Mode (⇧⌘S), Start a Meeting (⇧⌘M), Briefing (⇧⌘B), Open the page (⇧⌘P). While a sheet is open the commands for the window wait for it to close. Esc closes a sheet and the Type bar. Help, Pointe Help (⌘?) opens this page.

  • Uninstall Pointe… (in the Pointe menu): pointe uninstall's list, one question (your keys only if you tick their box), then it removes Pointe, the app included, and quits.

  • Update Pointe to shows at the top of the window when the standalone install in your home folder is older than the app: it runs the app's install.sh for its own version, as on the first launch.

  • Before Pointe runs, the window is the setup guide; Settings… (⌘,) opens this Mac's own settings over it, and About Pointe opens About.

  • The call bubble (menu or Settings): a small panel over other apps while a call is up, with the state, the talk mode and a talk button; if a call did not start, it says why. Click it to open the window; drag it anywhere, and it keeps the place. − makes it a small pill that opens out when the pointer is over it; × hides it ("Show Call Bubble During Calls" in the menu, or Settings, brings it back). Right-click it for Keep on Screen with No Call, Reset Position and the rest.

  • Alerts (Settings → Alerts): a notification when a session needs you and the window is not in front. Approve on a notification appears only for a request Pointe can read out and only with "Approve from notifications" on.

  • No Pointe on this Mac yet? The window is a setup guide then, with what is missing and the one thing to do about each, and Try a demo: invented sessions to look around, with nothing sent anywhere.

Drive mode starts on the phone or by voice; the Mac shows it and can stop it, as the page does.

Limits

  • A copy you built from source is signed for your own Mac only: another Mac refuses it. Pointe.dmg is the one to share.
  • One call at a time: hang up on the page or the phone to talk here.

  • Echo cancellation is macOS's own. If the audio device gives none, the call still starts and the window says to use headphones.

  • The microphone is the system's default one: there is no picker yet.

  • While your words show, the caption's wave animates and takes about a tenth of a processor core.
  • The app looks for no updates. When it and the plugin are at different versions, it says which side to update.

The signed disk image

tools/build_dmg.sh builds Pointe-<version>.dmg in one command: the app archived, signed with Developer ID through Xcode's account and notarized (Xcode exports it with destination "upload"), stapled; a disk image with an Applications link and the brand's background (tools/dmg_background.swift), itself signed, notarized and stapled. The steps around it, and the GitHub Actions job that does the same with secrets: releasing.md.
