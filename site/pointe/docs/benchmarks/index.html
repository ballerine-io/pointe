Benchmarks

The default models come from these runs. Scripts and reports are in bench/; re-run them with your own keys. All data is synthetic.

  • Brain and reader: bench/brain_bench.py, report in bench/results/brain.md. Real tool and speech path; 78 brain cases and 17 reader cases. On 78 cases with the daemon's own context block (2026-11-12), claude-sonnet-5-5 passes 91.9% ignoring length (95% CI 85.9% to 97.0%), median reply 1.8 s; its fallback claude-haiku-5-5 passes 88.0%; gemini-3.8-flash passes 94.9% (89.7% to 98.7%), median reply 2.7 s, with no unwanted sends against Sonnet's 9. The intervals overlap. Sonnet's 9 are all attempts to relay an approval that must be typed in the terminal; the daemon now refuses those in code, and the bench still counts the attempt. The earlier ranking on 66 cases, in bench/results/brain_substance.md, had Sonnet at 95.5%; nothing tested there beat it measurably, including Opus, Fable, high effort and 11 OpenAI models. Those prompts were tuned on Sonnet, which favours it. From that earlier run, not re-measured: gemini-4-argon 93.5% but 17 s.

  • Gate: tests/test_relevance_live.py, report in bench/results/gate.md.

  • Fully local (pointe local): the same benchmarks on an M5 Pro with 64 GB, 2026-11-13, report in bench/results/local.md. Brain gemma-4-26b-a4b-it (MLX 4-bit) passes 76.9% (67.9% to 85.5%) ignoring length, median reply 3.4 s, against 91.9% and 1.8 s for claude-sonnet-5-5; as the reader it passes 51.0% against 100% for Sonnet on 51 reports. Measured with other work running on the Mac.

  • Voice stays on your Mac (pointe local voice): local Whisper and Kokoro on MLX with the cloud brain, 2026-11-13, in docs/features/voice-stays-local.md. Kokoro on MLX, as the daemon runs it, starts English speech in 185 ms at the median (kokoro-onnx 750 ms in the same run); the whole setup passed 30 of 35 audio checks.

  • Which vendors and whole setups are covered, and what is not: bench/results/combinations.md.

  • Ears: bench/stt_extra.py, report in bench/results/report.txt. 70 utterances (English, Hebrew, mixed, short yes/no) x 4 voices x clean/noisy = 560 clips, at real-time pace, with a blind intent judge.

  • Mouth: bench/tts_bench.py, report in bench/results/tts.md. Time to first audio, plus intelligibility by round trip through a recognizer. Not covered: how it sounds to a person (samples are generated locally to listen to).

The ears run Soniox stt-rt-v6 since 2026-11-20 (a pre-release build) because Soniox removes stt-rt-v5 on 2026-12-15, not because v6 measured better. Until then v5 was the default: in the table, with Soniox's default endpoint settings, v6 never finalised 3 clips of 560 and waited over 1 s more often (17.6% of clips against 13.9%). A second run on 2026-11-20 with the daemon's own settings (bench/results/soniox-v6.md) found v6 as accurate (intent 95.9% against 94.6%, Hebrew 98.3% for both) with the same median endpoint (0.38 s against 0.36 s) and a much longer tail: p90 2.67 s against 0.82 s, Hebrew p90 3.04 s against 0.85 s, because on 35 of 560 clips v6 waits about 3.2 s before it ends the turn. Since 2026-11-20 (a pre-release build) the call asks Soniox for held words after 0.7 s of silence (turns.finalize_due): p90 0.76 s, Hebrew 0.80 s, no clip over 2.5 s, intent 96.1%; the cost is that a pause after a clause that reads whole no longer gets Soniox's occasional extra patience at Normal (60 of 60 such pauses taken, against 52 with v5).

Summary

The defaults come from benchmarks in bench/, all on synthetic data; re-run them with your own keys. The headline numbers:

  • Ears: 560 clips (English, Hebrew, mixed, short yes/no; 4 voices; clean and noisy). Soniox stt-rt-v6, what runs since Soniox removes v5 on 2026-12-15, got intent right 95.9% of the time, word error 0.063, latency p50 0.38 s; v5 had 95.4% and 0.36 s. v6 held some turns about 3 s; Pointe asks it for the words after a silence, which brings the p90 to 0.76 s (Hebrew 0.80 s). bench/results/report.txt, bench/results/soniox-v6.md

  • Brain: 78 cases on the real tool and speech path. claude-sonnet-5-5 passes 91.9% with a 1.8 s median reply; gemini-3.8-flash 94.9% at 2.7 s; the confidence intervals overlap. bench/results/brain.md

  • Gate: 24 cases, two runs per model; gemini-3.5-flash-lite failed none. bench/results/gate.md

  • Mouth: eleven_v4_turbo first audio 213 ms p50, word error 0.00 English, 0.02 Hebrew. bench/results/tts.md

  • Voice lock: equal error rate 1.9% on sentences, 2.7% on short answers, on 19 synthetic voices. bench/results/speaker.md

  • Vocabulary: a misheard name spelled right 25 of 64 times without hints, 60 of 64 with them. bench/results/vocab.md

  • Challenge me: 15/30 to 30/30 on Sonnet, 21/30 to 30/30 on Gemini Flash. bench/results/challenge.md

  • Whisper: Soniox word error 1.4% on a synthetic whisper at -18 dB, 18.4% at -30 dB. bench/results/whisper.md

  • Fully local, on an M5 Pro: brain 76.9% with a 3.4 s median reply, reader 51.0%, ears 85.7% intent at 0.76 s, Kokoro's first English audio 1.3 s, 10 of 19 single-session audio checks. bench/results/local.md

  • Voice stays on your Mac, on an M5 Pro: Kokoro on MLX starts English speech in 185 ms at the median (kokoro-onnx 750 ms, ElevenLabs 213 ms); Whisper large-v3-turbo got 82.7% of intents in the same run as Soniox's 94.6%; 30 of 35 audio checks. docs/features/voice-stays-local.md

  • Which setups were tested end to end: bench/results/combinations.md

Not measured for any setup: a real human voice on a real call.

Conversational engineering benchmarks. Five benchmarks of what talking costs a coding agent, led by Humanity's Last Keystroke (small coding tasks finished by voice only), with one fixed agent and synthetic speech: docs/benchmarks/, and on the site. In the first run Pointe did not beat the LLM-router baseline we wrote on any of them, and fell well below it on most. Pointe fixed the defects it found and its arm was run again: it gained on routing (41 to 49 of 60 turns), identifiers (40 to 42 of 48) and spoken answers (18 to 23 of 27), stayed below the baseline on most, and lost on HLK (34 to 27 of 72), a rerun during which the brain model was often slow. The pages show both runs, by how much and why.

Cost arithmetic

A busy hour on the best setup: brain 60 replies at $0.0045, about $0.27. Mouth 60 replies of 30 words, about 11,000 characters at $0.04 per 1,000, about $0.43. Reader 10 reports at $0.01, about $0.10. Soniox $0.12 per stream hour with up to two streams, about $0.24. About $1 in all. Prices read 2026-11-06; check them. This is arithmetic, not a metered bill.
