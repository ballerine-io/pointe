Wrist approvals and desktop alerts

A session needs you: your watch taps, it shows what the session wants to run, and you deny or, if you allow it, approve from the wrist. On the desktop, the same event is a notification that brings the page forward.

This is version 1: iPhone notifications that iOS mirrors to an Apple Watch, and browser notifications on the Mac. There is no native watchOS app yet. The rules live in pointe/alerts.py; pointe/daemon.py builds the attention list from them and checks an Approve that comes from a notification.

Use it

  • Page: Settings → Alerts → "Desktop alerts" (per browser; the browser asks for permission when you tick it). An item that appears while the tab is hidden becomes one notification. It closes when the item is answered or the tab is shown; a click brings the page forward. Approving stays a click on the card.

  • iPhone: Menu → Alerts → "Alerts on this iPhone" (off until you turn it on). The app posts a local notification when a session needs you and the app is not on screen, and withdraws it when the item is answered. iOS mirrors it to a watch on your wrist while the iPhone is locked.

  • Mac app: Settings (••• at the top, or ⌘,) → Alerts → "Alerts on this Mac". A notification when a session needs you and the window is not in front, with Deny on it. The app keeps checking from the menu bar, so alerts arrive with the window closed. A click opens the window.

  • "Approve from notifications" (one setting for every client, off by default; the iPhone app asks for Face ID and the Mac app for Touch ID or your password to turn it on) adds Approve to a notification. On the Mac, a locked Mac asks to be unlocked first.

  • Voice: no phrase; these are settings. On a call the voice itself reads a request out, and tells you when a session waits at a prompt in its terminal (below).

What raises an alert: a permission request, a Claude Code session stopped for 15 seconds at a prompt of its own in its terminal (a permission prompt, a question, a sandbox request: the ones the voice does not hold, for example with no call up), a session that ended its turn waiting on you, a raised hand in a meeting, a conflict, and a scheduled briefing that is ready.

Rules

  • What a notification shows is the read-out form of a request (the read-out allowlist), never its full text or the model's description. That text may hold a secret. A request that cannot be read out shows only "<Tool>: open Pointe to see the exact request". A credential-like value is never shown or said.

  • Approve from a notification is offered only when the setting is on and the request is one Pointe can read out: exactly what a spoken yes could approve. It needs an unlocked iPhone, or a watch on the wrist, instead of Face ID. The daemon accepts it (via: "notification") only for that request's id. Anything else needs the app (Face ID) or the page.

  • Deny is always offered for a permission, and is the first action.

  • A prompt in a session's terminal has no actions and fixed words ("A permission prompt in its terminal. Answer it there."): Pointe does not hold that prompt, so nothing here could answer it. One you answer at the keyboard within 15 seconds never alerts, and a dialog you may have opened yourself never does. On a call, in orchestrator mode, the voice says it once the room is quiet, like any session stuck on you.

  • Quiet while you are at the Mac. The phone, and the page opened on another device, post nothing while this Mac had keyboard, mouse or trackpad input in the last 60 seconds (IOKit's HIDIdleTime, read with ioreg at most every 5 seconds while a client asks). The item is held, not dropped: it is posted on the first check after you leave the Mac. An approval within 60 seconds of going back to its terminal is never held. A minute covers reading between keystrokes and lets the next alert through soon after you walk away; a missed approval costs more than one extra buzz. The page on this Mac is never held: its desktop alerts are for the person at the Mac. An idle time that could not be read, or is over 15 seconds old, counts as away.

  • A stale request answers "Already answered"; a refused Approve answers "Approve it in the app"; anything else answers "Mac not reachable", and the notification is posted again on the next check.

Limits

  • No push server. The phone learns about a request from its own status checks. This works while the app is alive: open, or in the background with a call up. With the call down and the app in the background, iOS checks only when it grants a Background App Refresh: rarely, never in Low Power Mode, never after you swipe the app away.

  • Checked by hand on a real iPhone and watch (the simulator has no watch); the steps are in The iPhone app.

  • Not built: per-event switches, sound and lock-screen preview settings (iOS's own settings cover them), Touch ID on the page, and the Time Sensitive entitlement.

API

  • Status attention: [{id, kind: permission|terminal|question|hand|conflict|briefing, session?, request_id?, title, body, text, approvable, quiet, at}]. quiet: the user is at this Mac and the client asking is not on it, so it posts nothing for the item yet. Status mac_idle_secs: seconds since the Mac's last input, or null when unknown. A terminal item is a prompt in the session's own terminal, id terminal:<session>:<since>. title and body are what a notification may show. approvable: a notification may offer Approve. A question is a session that ended its turn waiting on you, id question:<session>:<since>.

  • POST /voice/approve takes via: "notification": an Allow is refused (403) unless the setting is on and the request is approvable; a stale request_id is 409. Deny needs neither.

  • POST /voice/attention {id} dismisses one item for this call.

  • Pref alerts.watch_approve (default false) in GET/POST /voice/prefs.
