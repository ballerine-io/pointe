Push to talk

Press a key or a button to be heard. Otherwise nothing is heard, transcribed or sent to any vendor. For open offices, noisy rooms, meetings, and anyone who does not want an open microphone.

The state lives in pointe/ptt.py; the gate (PttGate) is in pointe/daemon.py. The two ways to press are designed in docs/design/push-to-talk-modes.md.

Use it

The switch above the mic ("Always on" or "Hold to talk", which reads "Tap to talk" when that is the talk button), on the page, in the iPhone app and in the Mac app; or Settings → Microphone ("Always on" or "Push to talk"). In the apps, Settings is the ••• button at the top (on the Mac also ⌘,). It is one daemon setting, so every client agrees. No spoken phrase switches it: a press is what opens the microphone. Then Talk button, each device's own choice: Hold to talk (the default: press and hold, let go to send) or Tap to talk (tap to start, the microphone stays open, tap again to send).

The Mac app draws the iPhone app's screens, so the mic ring, the switch, the two talk buttons and the draft are the same there. Its own ways to press: Space while the window is in front, and the talk shortcut from any app (Settings → This Mac → Talk shortcut; ⌃⌥Space until you record another). The shortcut is a real hold: macOS tells the app when the key goes down and when it comes up, with no Accessibility or Input Monitoring permission. In Always on, Space and the shortcut mute; with no call up, the shortcut brings Pointe forward. The talk mode and the shortcut are named at the top of the window and in the call bubble. An AirPods press does not reach the Mac app: macOS gives it to the app that owns the media keys.

A press is one message, like a voice message: nothing you say while the button is open is a turn. Pause as long as you like, start over, say "hold on" or "brain dump" mid-thought: none of it is acted on until you let go (Hold to talk) or tap again (Tap to talk). Then all of it goes to Pointe as one turn, at once, without the "Wait before answering" delay, since the release already says you are done. A press with nothing heard sends nothing. Always on is unchanged: there a pause after a sentence still ends the turn (turn taking).

On the page, a headset or AirPods button press reaches it as a media key (play/pause) and toggles talking, or mutes in Always on. The system sends play/pause by itself too: when the call connects or media starts, on an audio route change (a headset joins or leaves, an AirPod comes out), often several in a second. The page takes a media key as a press only when it comes alone (nothing else within 0.3 s, so a press acts 0.3 s late) and not within 1.5 s of any of those; an ignored one extends the 1.5 s. Each is logged as a page event, ignored or not.

A press under 0.25 s in Hold mode heard no word; the line says "Hold to talk · or choose Tap to talk in Settings" for two seconds. A tap forgotten open closes after 90 s by the daemon's rule below; the line says "Talk closed after 90 s: tap again".

Talk from any app

The page's keys work only while its tab is focused: a browser gets no key events otherwise. To talk from whatever app you are in, bind a system-wide shortcut to the daemon's CLI; the page or the phone app stays in the background with the call up.

  1. Settings → Microphone on the page shows the command and copies it: ~/.claude-voice/bin/pointe talk. That launcher is written by the daemon at each start and runs the Pointe it runs from, so the shortcut survives a plugin update (the plugin cache path changes with each version).

  2. In the Shortcuts app: new shortcut → action Run Shell Script → paste the command.

  3. Open the shortcut's details (ⓘ) → Add Keyboard Shortcut, for example ⌃⌥Space. Shortcuts' keyboard shortcuts are system-wide; nothing else to install, no permission to grant.

Press once to talk, again to stop: the shortcut is a toggle, not a hold. Seeing a key held from any app needs an event tap (Input Monitoring or Accessibility permission, a keylogger-class grant), which Pointe does not ask for. The daemon's own limits still apply: a press forgotten open closes after 90 s, as Caps Lock does. For a real hold from any app, a tool that sees key down and key up (Raycast, Hammerspoon, BetterTouchTool) runs pointe talk on on the press and pointe talk off on the release.

The CLI prints one line and exits 0, or 1 with the reason: "Microphone is Always on: set it to Push to talk in Settings", "No call is up: press Connect on the page or in the app first", "Pointe is not running on this Mac".

On the iPhone the same thing is Talk to Pointe, and it does more: with no call up it starts one. See On the iPhone, from outside the app.

Either way the gate on the Mac is pressed, so the rules below hold, and the client that holds the call is told to send its audio (it sends only while a press holds, its own or the shortcut's).

On the iPhone, from outside the app

One press of Talk to Pointe opens Pointe talking to the orchestrator: from the Action button, Control Center, the Lock Screen or the Home Screen. With no call up it calls your Mac first. Press again, or tap the mic, to send.

Add it (once):

  1. Action button (iPhone 15 Pro and later): the iPhone's Settings, Action Button, swipe to Controls, Choose a Control, Talk to Pointe.

  2. Control Center: swipe down from the top right, hold an empty spot, Add a Control, search "Pointe", tap Talk to Pointe.

  3. Lock Screen: hold the Lock Screen, Customize, Lock Screen, tap a control at the bottom (the flashlight or the camera), remove it with −, tap +, Talk to Pointe.

  4. Home Screen: hold an empty spot, Edit, Add Widget, search "Pointe", Add Widget.

Siri ("Talk to Pointe") and Back Tap (Settings, Accessibility, Touch, Back Tap, then the Talk to Pointe shortcut) press it too.

What a press does (TalkLaunch in apps/PointeKit, carried out by PhoneTalk in PointeUI):

The call Microphone A press
none opens Pointe, dials, opens a press as soon as the call is up ("Talking · tap to send"), and puts the Mac in Orchestrator mode if it was in Session mode
dialling opens Pointe; nothing more
up, no press Push to talk opens Pointe in Orchestrator mode, with a press open
up, a press open Push to talk sends it, as releasing the mic does
up Always on opens Pointe; it is already listening, and a press never hangs up

In the app the line says Talking · tap to send and the mic is orange: a tap on it sends, whichever talk button you chose. A locked iPhone asks you to unlock first, as for opening any app, so nothing listens before you have.

Leaving the app. With the call up, go Home or lock the phone: the Lock Screen and the Dynamic Island show it, with Send and Cancel while a press is open, then Talk and Hang up, and the count of sessions that need you. Send and Hang up work without unlocking. Cancel and Hang up end the call, and a press still open is never sent: the Mac sends a press only after its release, and a call that ends first never releases it (tests/test_ptt_message.py).

The widget is one Talk to Pointe button. Where the app group exists (a paid developer account, the App Store build) it also shows whether your Mac is reachable and how many sessions need you, as the app last saw them, with the time. It never shows a session's name or a request's text, and it holds no key.

On a free Apple Account. Everything above works except the widget's status. The widget reads it from an app group the app shares with its extension, and a free Personal Team's provisioning profiles carry no App Groups (Apple's capability table lists them for free accounts, but on a real phone the profiles came back without them, for any group name). So the project signs the group in only when the build asks (POINTE_SHARE_GROUP=YES; a plain Xcode build does not). pointe apps ios asks for it first and, when the profile lacks it, builds again without it and says so; the App Store build (apps/ios/release.sh) and the Simulator always have it. Without it the widget is the plain button.

Approving is unchanged. Nothing on this path approves a request: it dials, sets the mode, opens and closes the press, and hangs up (tests/test_widget.py holds the code to that). Approve still needs Face ID; a spoken yes still passes every rule of the voice lock and the read-back.

Why it opens the app. 1.24.0 recorded from the background (an audio recording intent under a Live Activity), without opening Pointe. In use it was not good enough: the first press waited for iOS to launch the app in the background with nothing on screen (about 9 s in the simulator before the app's first request), Send lived in the Dynamic Island or on the Lock Screen, Live Activities had to be allowed first, and a locked phone needed the voice lock on Commands or Everything or an extra unlock step. In front, the microphone starts at once, Send is the mic under your thumb, and the lock is the iPhone's own. From the moment the app runs to the press being open on the Mac took 0.7 to 1.0 s in the simulator (apps/ios/e2e/talk_outside_ui.py prints it); how long iOS takes to bring the app up is the phone's, and on a phone it is well under the simulator's.

Why not Apple's Push to Talk framework. It gives a real system hold-to-talk, but it is built for talking to a group over a channel: incoming audio arrives as an Apple push the sender's server sends, which a daemon on your own Mac cannot send without Apple's push credentials, and its entitlement and push notifications are not available to a free developer account. A Control cannot be held either, so a press opens and the next one sends.

How it is built. The intents (apps/ios/PointeShared/TalkIntents.swift) are compiled into the app and into the widget extension (apps/ios/PointeWidget: the Control, the widget, the Live Activity). TalkIntent opens the app (openAppWhenRun); the Live Activity's Send and Cancel are Live Activity intents, which iOS performs in the app's process without opening it. The app owns the one call (PhoneTalk), so the press, the call screen and the Live Activity act on the same call.

Tested. apps/PointeKit/Tests/PointeKitTests/TalkLaunchTests.swift holds the rules. apps/ios/e2e/talk_outside_ui.py runs against a stand-in Mac (talk_mac.py) in the simulator: the real Control Center control and the real Home Screen widget with the app not running (Pointe opens talking, the mic sends, Hang up ends the call), and the Lock Screen's Send and Hang up; the Mac must see the press open, Orchestrator mode before the send, the press closed, the hang-up, and no approval. The simulator has no Action button, no real microphone and no Face ID lock, so these are tried on a phone:

  1. Add Talk to Pointe to the Action button. Unlocked, no call: press it. Pointe opens, "Talking · tap to send"; say a sentence; press again (or tap the mic). It is sent as one turn and answered; the Mac is in Orchestrator mode.

  2. Lock the iPhone, press the Action button: it asks for Face ID, then step 1 happens.

  3. With the call up, go Home: the Dynamic Island shows it; lock: Send, then Talk, then Hang up from the Lock Screen.
  4. Add the Control Center control and the Home Screen widget; each does step 1.
  5. Settings → Talk from outside the app off: a press with no call opens Pointe and says the setting is off; on a call it still talks.

What the daemon enforces

  • The gate sits first after the transport, before the voice detector, the ears and any realtime model. Closed, it drops the microphone audio. Whatever a client sends, no audio passes it.

  • While a press is open (and until it is sent) every final transcript is kept, not answered: no settle time, no hold phrase, no command, no reply, and no news spoken over it. Interim words and kept finals show as the draft.

  • On release it keeps 0.4 s more, plus 0.2 s of link slack, so your last word is not cut, then asks the ears to finish the utterance. Soniox answers with the last words and a <fin> marker; once that marker is in, everything said in the press is one turn. Ears that give no marker get 2 s (FINAL_WAIT_SECS), then what was heard is sent.

  • A second press before the first was sent (within that second or so) continues the same message.

  • A press closes by itself after 90 s, or after 2 s with no audio (a lost release). The 90 s limit applies to toggles (Caps Lock, AirPods) as well as holds, because the daemon cannot tell them apart. When a press closes by itself, the clients drop the hold and say why: held too long, no audio.

  • A new call starts closed.

  • A press while the bot speaks interrupts it and cuts any read-out. For the approval rules, your utterance begins at the press.

  • A press counts as activity for the idle hang-up.

During a meeting

A meeting you started (it asks for consent and shows a recording indicator) sends the room's audio to the meeting's speech stream without a press. Talking to Pointe outside the meeting stream still needs one. The status says so (ptt.room_heard: true).

On the iPhone

In a voice-chat audio session, iOS delivers an AirPods stem press as an input mute, not as play/pause. In push to talk, the app takes that mute as the toggle and unmutes the input again. It also handles play/pause in case iOS sends it.

The microphone runs for the whole call, in push to talk too; the app sends nothing while the gate is closed and keeps only the last 1.5 s in memory. A press sends at once, right after its press message, the audio from 0.1 s before its touch-down, then the live audio: the first word is not lost to the time the touch took to reach the gate, or to the wait for the microphone's next buffer (TalkSender.swift). Nothing older than that 0.1 s ever leaves the phone. Measured on the simulator with apps/ios/e2e/ptt_latency.py (the real app and microphone, a stand-in Mac running ptt.Ptt): the first chunk left 83 to 94 ms after the touch (it was 108 to 191 ms), and a press's audio now starts 100 ms before the touch (it started 0 to 84 ms after it, so that much of the first word was lost).

API

  • Setting: "microphone": "always" | "ptt" in GET/POST /voice/prefs (default always).

  • Control, client text on the call socket: {"type": "ptt", "open": true|false, "at": <client ms>}. Ignored unless the setting is ptt.

  • From outside the call: POST /voice/talk {"action": "on" | "off" | "toggle"} (default toggle), with the page's key or the CLI token ($STATE/token), never a phone key. 409 with a plain-words detail when the microphone is Always on or no call is up; otherwise the gate is pressed as a client's message would press it, the client gets {"type": "ptt", "open": bool, "by": "shortcut"} on the call socket and sends accordingly, and the reply is {"open": bool, "mode": "ptt"}. GET /voice/status carries talk_command (the command to bind) on this Mac only.

  • Status ptt: {mode, open, opened_at, closed_at, closed_why, sent_bytes, room_heard, room_bytes, draft}. draft: true while a press's words are kept and not sent yet; hearing is then all of them so far, however long the pause. closed_why: released, held too long, no audio, switched to push to talk. sent_bytes counts audio that passed the gate in this call. Clients show open as the truth.
