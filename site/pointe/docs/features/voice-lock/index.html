Voice lock

Only your voice commands Pointe. Someone else in the room (a colleague, a TV) is heard by the microphone but does not count. Pointe already requires a bound yes for approvals; the voice lock adds "and it was you".

The rules live in pointe/speaker.py. The daemon asks it on each final utterance (SonioxEars._owner_gate) and on each yes (on_user_utterance).

Use it

  • Voice: no phrase turns it on or sets a level. Enrolment listens on the call: you read the lines it shows aloud.
  • Page: Settings → Voice lock → Enrol my voice, then the level.
  • iPhone app: Settings (••• at the top) → Voice lock.
  • Mac app: Settings (••• at the top, or ⌘,) → Voice lock.

Enrol during a call: Pointe asks you to read a few lines, and learns those utterances instead of answering them. Each device (the Mac's microphone, the iPhone) gets its own voiceprint; scoring compares against every voiceprint and the best one wins.

Level What it does
Off No check.
Approvals A spoken yes counts only in your voice. A no counts from anyone. The default after the first enrolment.
Commands Other voices never reach the voice model. They are listed as ignored, with their words, in memory only.
Everything The same, and their words are not shown or kept. Live captions stay empty until the final words pass the check.

Deleting the last voiceprint turns the lock off. The iPhone app asks for Face ID, and the Mac app for Touch ID or your password, before it lowers the level or deletes a voiceprint.

The score of each spoken answer to a request is kept in the approvals record.

Rules

  • It only narrows. A match is never consent. Every approval rule still has to hold for your yes; the lock adds "and it was your voice" and removes nothing.

  • It fails closed. No model yet, a model error, a score that takes longer than 0.6 s, under 0.3 s of audio, or a damaged voiceprint file: the speech counts as not yours for what the level guards. The page and the app say why. A tap, Face ID, the terminal and typed text still work, so a failure costs convenience, never control. The lock is not silently lowered when it cannot score: you turned it on, and a silent downgrade would lift it without your knowing.

  • Short words. A "yes" is too short to score well. It counts on its own only after a strong match (score ≥ 0.55 with ≥ 1 s of speech) within the last minute, on the same device's voiceprint. Continuity never chains from another weak match.

  • Commands and Everything gate at intake, before the voice model hears the words. Typed text is authenticated by the page key or the phone key and is not gated.

  • Barge-in. Anyone can still cut the bot off at once (scoring needs about a second of audio). When the lock keeps that voice's words out, the interrupted report comes back at once.

  • Push to talk. Only speech is scored, never the silence between a press and your first word.

  • Meetings. With a voiceprint and the lock on, a meeting turn in your voice marks you as the owner of that speaker label, and a "that's me" in another voice is refused. With the lock off, only the words that started the meeting or a tap on "That was me" mark the owner; a spoken "that's me" never does.

  • Drive mode uses the voiceprint whenever one is stored, at any level: see drive mode.

  • Whisper mode never lowers the lock: see whisper mode.

Privacy

The voiceprint is biometric data. It is computed and kept only on this Mac, in ~/.claude-voice/voiceprint/ (0700, one file per device, 0600), and deleted from Settings → Voice lock. No vendor sees the audio for this check or the voiceprint. The journal records an ignored utterance's length and score, never its words.

The model is 3D-Speaker ERes2Net base (Apache-2.0, 40 MB), run by sherpa-onnx. It is downloaded on first use from the sherpa-onnx GitHub release into ~/.claude-voice/models/, checked against a pinned SHA-256. See THIRD_PARTY.md.

It is not authentication

It is a check against the bystander, not an attacker. A recording of your voice can pass it.

Measured

bench/speaker_bench.py, report in bench/results/speaker.md: 19 synthetic macOS voices, each the owner once and an impostor for the others. Equal error rate 1.9% on 2 to 4 s sentences, 2.7% on short answers ("yes", "go ahead"). A CAM++ model scored about 32% on sentences on the same bench, so ERes2Net was chosen.

The thresholds (T_HIGH = 0.55, T_LOW = 0.35) come from synthetic voices only. Synthetic voices are steadier than people. A bench on recorded people (several microphones, a cold, real impostors) has not been run, so Approvals is the level to use; Commands and Everything are not yet recommended on that evidence.

Not built

A profile for the bot's own voice as an impostor (the echo guard covers echo), adapting the voiceprint over time, a reminder to enrol, and a "you sound different today" prompt.

API

POST /voice/voiceprint (page cookie, bearer token, phone key), returns {voice_lock: <status>}:

Body Does Errors
{action: "enrol", device} arms enrolment on the live call 409: no call, a meeting on, native ears
{action: "cancel"} drops an unfinished enrolment
{action: "delete", device?} deletes one device's voiceprint, or all
{action: "level", level} off, approvals, commands, everything 400 bad level; 409 without a voiceprint

The level is the pref voice_lock, readable at GET /voice/prefs. POST /voice/prefs refuses it, so there is one write path, where the voiceprint is checked.

Status voice_lock: {level, enrolled, devices: [{device, seconds, segments, enrolled_at}], enrolling, lines, model: missing|downloading|ready|failed, problem, ignored: {count, last_at, recent: [{at, secs, text?}]}, speaker: null | {owner, score, at}, unsure_streak}. text in ignored.recent appears only at Commands.
